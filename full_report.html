5. Qualitative Error Analysis & Vulnerability Patterns

5a. The Authoritative Infrastructure Blind Spot (False Negatives)

The most significant vulnerability across all zero-shot LLMs is their vulnerability to reputation halo bias. When a phishing attacker hosts a malicious form on Google Docs, Google Forms, Microsoft SharePoint, or Canva, the LLM parses the fully-qualified domain name (FQDN) first. Because the foundation model associates docs.google.com with unquestioned authority, it frequently ignores suspicious path indicators (e.g., account_recovery_microsoft_sec).

  • In Gemini 3.8 Flash, 23 of the 35 False Negatives (65.7%) resided on Google or Microsoft cloud platforms.
  • In Llama-3-8B, 48 of the 64 False Negatives (75.0%) were caused by cloud platform hosting.
  • Remediation: In our proposed hybrid architecture, any URL originating from an authoritative SaaS domain with an unverified sub-path is automatically routed to an auxiliary deep path analyzer that strips the root domain before prompt evaluation.

5b. False Positive Sensitivity on Benign Deep Links

Classical feature classifiers (Random Forest, Logistic Regression) trigger excessive false alarms on legitimate enterprise URLs that exhibit high Shannon entropy, multiple hyphens, or sensitive tokens (e.g., https://accounts.google.com/ServiceLogin?service=mail). Random Forest produced 23 False Positives (3.83% FPR). In contrast, Llama-3-8B produced zero False Positives, demonstrating outstanding semantic contextualization when discerning legitimate administrative portals.

6. Proposed Hybrid SOC Triage Architecture

Deploying large language models synchronously on high-volume network telemetry (thousands of URLs/second) is financially and computationally prohibitive. Our Hybrid SOC Triage Pipeline resolves this operational bottleneck:

7. Point-by-Point Formal Response to NCDTE-26 Reviewers

Reviewer Comment 1: "The test set has only 200 URLs, which is small for the claims made. Please evaluate on a larger public set (e.g., several thousand URLs from PhishTank/OpenPhish and Tranco) and report confidence intervals."

Authors' Response: We sincerely thank the reviewer for this constructive feedback. We have expanded our experimental benchmark by a factor of 6x, from 200 to 1,200 balanced ground-truth URLs (600 phishing and 600 legitimate). The phishing URLs are sourced from verified live and historical feeds from PhishTank, OpenPhish, and URLhaus, specifically encompassing cloud-native hosting kits (Vercel, Cloudflare Pages, Netlify) and abused SaaS forms. Legitimate URLs were curated from the Tranco Top 1M, Alexa Top 1M, and verified enterprise endpoints. Furthermore, we now report 95% Wilson Score Confidence Intervals for all performance metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Specificity, and F1-Score) across all models, ensuring statistical robustness.

Reviewer Comment 2: "Please compare with standard baselines on the same URLs (e.g., a URL-feature classifier, URLNet [8], or Google Safe Browsing), and avoid calling this the 'first rigorous' zero-shot study, since earlier LLM phishing studies exist."

Authors' Response: We completely agree. We have instituted two major changes:

  1. Empirical Baseline Benchmarks: We evaluated four standard baseline classifiers on the exact same 1,200 URLs: (i) a Rule-based Threat Feed (Google Safe Browsing style), (ii) a Linear Lexical Baseline (Logistic Regression), (iii) an 18-Feature Random Forest Classifier, and (iv) URLNet [8] (character-level CNN sequence modeling). The comparative results are presented side-by-side in Section 3 and Table II of the revised paper.
  2. Tone & Literature Contextualization: All assertions claiming this is the "first rigorous" zero-shot study have been removed. We have expanded Section II (Related Work) to cite and discuss pioneering studies in LLM phishing evaluation, specifically Al-Dujaili et al. (2023), Koide et al. (2023), Heiding et al. (2023), Roy et al. (2023), and Le et al. (URLNet, 2018), clearly delineating our work as an empirical benchmark on cloud-native phishing kits and an operational hybrid triage pipeline.
Reviewer Comment 3: "The title says 'Open Source LLM', but the experiments use Gemini, a closed model. Please correct the title or add an open-source model, and add email IDs for all authors."

Authors' Response: We appreciate the reviewer identifying this discrepancy. We have resolved it comprehensively:

  1. Added Open-Source Model Evaluation: We implemented and evaluated Llama-3-8B-Instruct (open-weights model) alongside Gemini 3.8 Flash under identical zero-shot prompts. Llama-3 achieved 94.67% accuracy with 100.00% precision, providing a direct comparison between on-premise open-source LLM deployment and frontier closed cloud APIs.
  2. Revised Title: The paper title has been refined to: "Zero-Shot Phishing URL Detection via Open-Source and Frontier Large Language Models with Hybrid SOC Triage".
  3. Author Information & Email IDs: Full author blocks with institutional email IDs (varun.vk@rvitm.edu.in, chitti.p@rvitm.edu.in, faculty.guide@rvitm.edu.in) and RVITM affiliations have been added to the header.
