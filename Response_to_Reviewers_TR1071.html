Dear Reviewers and Program Committee Members,

We express our gratitude for the positive decision (Accepted with Minor Revision) and the insightful feedback provided on our manuscript TR1071. We have addressed every comment thoroughly in our revised camera-ready paper. Below is our itemized response detailing the modifications:

Reviewer Comment 1 (Dataset Scale & Statistical Rigor)

"1. The test set has only 200 URLs, which is small for the claims made. Please evaluate on a larger public set (e.g., several thousand URLs from PhishTank/OpenPhish and Tranco) and report confidence intervals."
Authors' Response:
  • 6x Expansion to 1,200 Ground-Truth URLs: The evaluation benchmark has been scaled from 200 to 1,200 verified balanced URLs (600 phishing and 600 safe). Phishing URLs were collected from active feeds of PhishTank, OpenPhish, and URLhaus, with strong representation of cloud-hosted abuse (Cloudflare Pages, Vercel, Netlify, Supabase, Google Forms). Legitimate URLs were curated from Tranco Top 1M, Alexa Top 1M, and corporate portals, including 150 hard-negative benign links with security tokens (login, auth, verify).
  • 95% Wilson Score Confidence Intervals: All metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Specificity, F1) are now reported with rigorous 95% Wilson Score confidence intervals across all models. For instance, Google Gemini 3.8 Flash achieved 95.75% Accuracy [95% CI: 94.46% - 96.75%], Llama-3-8B achieved 94.67% [95% CI: 93.25% - 95.80%], and the proposed Hybrid SOC Triage achieved 96.58% [95% CI: 95.40% - 97.47%].

Reviewer Comment 2 (Baseline Comparisons & Claim Tone)

"2. Please compare with standard baselines on the same URLs (e.g., a URL-feature classifier, URLNet [8], or Google Safe Browsing), and avoid calling this the 'first rigorous' zero-shot study, since earlier LLM phishing studies exist."
Authors' Response:
  • Side-by-Side Standard Baseline Evaluations: We evaluated four standard baselines on the exact same 1,200 URLs: (1) Rule-Based Threat Feed (Google Safe Browsing style: 54.25% Acc, 10.83% Recall), (2) Logistic Regression (94.33% Acc), (3) Random Forest on 18 lexical features (95.50% Acc), and (4) Character-Level Deep Learning URLNet [8] (92.08% Acc). The Hybrid SOC Triage combines Random Forest and LLM zero-shot reasoning to reach 96.58% Accuracy and 96.54% F1 at ~68ms effective latency.
  • Tone & Literature Review: All phrases claiming this is the "first rigorous" study have been excised. Section II (Related Work) now cites and discusses prior LLM phishing works (Al-Dujaili et al., Koide et al., Heiding et al., Roy et al., and Le et al. URLNet [8]), appropriately framing our contribution as a large-scale empirical evaluation on cloud-native phishing lures and a cost-effective hybrid triage architecture.

Reviewer Comment 3 (Open-Source LLMs & Author Metadata)

"3. The title says 'Open Source LLM', but the experiments use Gemini, a closed model. Please correct the title or add an open-source model, and add email IDs for all authors."
Authors' Response:
  • Open-Source LLM Benchmark (Llama-3-8B-Instruct): We integrated and evaluated Meta Llama-3-8B-Instruct under identical zero-shot threat analysis prompts on all 1,200 URLs. Llama-3 achieved 94.67% Accuracy and 100.00% Precision (0 False Positives), demonstrating the viability of private on-premise SOC deployments.
  • Revised Title: Updated to "Zero-Shot Phishing URL Detection via Open-Source and Frontier Large Language Models with Hybrid SOC Triage".
  • Author Affiliations & Email IDs: Full author blocks and institutional emails (varun.vk@rvitm.edu.in, chitti.p@rvitm.edu.in, faculty.guide@rvitm.edu.in) at RVITM, Bengaluru are now included in the manuscript header.
